# 3. Stop-loss and take-profit

*A plan has two exits written down before you enter: where you're wrong, and where you take profit.*

### Stop-loss

A stop-loss sells automatically if the price falls to your level. Put it where your idea is *proven wrong*, for example just under a recent low or a support level, not at a random round number.

### Take-profit

A take-profit sells automatically at your target, for example near the next resistance. It stops you from giving back gains out of greed.

### Rules that help

- Never move a stop further away to “give it room”. That's hoping, not planning.
- You may move a stop up once the trade is in profit (a **trailing** stop) to protect gains.
- Leave room for normal noise: if a coin moves 4% on a quiet day, a 1% stop will get hit by random wiggles.

> [!example]
> BTC at €67,000. The last swing low is €64,500. A stop at €64,300 (−4%) sits under that low. A target at €72,000 (+7.5%) is the next resistance.

## Check yourself

**Where is a sensible place for a stop-loss?**
- Exactly at a round number like €60,000
- Just below a recent swing low or support
- As far away as possible so it never triggers

> [!success]- Answer
> Just below a recent swing low or support. If price breaks the level your idea depends on, the idea is wrong. Round numbers attract stop hunts.

**Your trade is losing and close to the stop. What do you do?**
- Move the stop lower to give it room
- Keep the stop where you planned it
- Remove the stop and wait

> [!success]- Answer
> Keep the stop where you planned it. Moving stops away turns small planned losses into big unplanned ones.
