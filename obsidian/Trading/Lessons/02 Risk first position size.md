# 2. Risk first: position size

*Professional traders think about how much they can lose before how much they can win. The size of a position matters more than the coin.*

### The 1–2% rule

Decide how much of your account you're willing to lose on one trade, usually **1–2%**. With €10,000 that's €100–€200. That is the most a single trade may cost you if it goes wrong.

### From risk to position size

**Position size = money at risk ÷ distance to your stop-loss.**

> [!example]
> Account €10,000, you accept €150 risk (1.5%). You plan a stop-loss 6% below your entry. Position size = €150 ÷ 0.06 = **€2,500**. If the stop hits, you lose about €150, not more.

### Why it matters

After a 50% loss you need +100% just to get back to even. Small, controlled losses keep you in the game long enough to learn and to catch the good trades.

- Lose 10% → need +11% to recover
- Lose 25% → need +33%
- Lose 50% → need +100%

## Check yourself

**Account €5,000, risk 2%, stop 10% below entry. Position size?**
- €500
- €1,000
- €100

> [!success]- Answer
> €1,000. 2% of €5,000 = €100; €100 ÷ 0.10 = €1,000.

**You're down 50%. What gain gets you back to your starting balance?**
- +50%
- +75%
- +100%

> [!success]- Answer
> +100%. Half your money must double to return to where you started.
